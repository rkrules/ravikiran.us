## Publications

**"Power Analysis is Essential: High-Powered Tests Suggest Minimal to No Effect of Rounded Shapes on Click-Through Rates"**
*Econ Journal Watch*, Vol. 23, No. 1, March 2026, pp. 139–172

**Authors:** Ron Kohavi, Jakub Linowski, Lukas Vermeer, Fabrice Boisseranc, Joachim Furuseth, Andrew Gelman, Guido Imbens, **Ravikiran Rajagopal**

**Ravikiran's affiliation listed:** Director of Product Management, United Parks & Resorts

**DOI / URL:** https://econjwatch.org/file_download/1407/KohaviEtAlMarch2026.pdf

**What the paper is:** A peer-reviewed empirical replication study in a scholarly economics journal. The paper challenges a prior *Journal of Consumer Research* claim that rounded UI shapes increase click-through rates by 55%. The authors ran three high-powered AEA-registered A/B tests — one on the SeaWorld Orlando website (Ravikiran's platform) and two on Coop Norway sites — with 2.8M, 2.2M, and 1.9M users respectively. Results showed minimal to no effect (effect sizes of 0.16%, 0.29%, 0.73%), directly contradicting the original claim. The paper makes the case that power analysis is essential to credible online experimentation.

**Notable co-authors:**
- **Ron Kohavi** — Pioneer of online controlled experimentation; former VP at Microsoft, Amazon, and Airbnb; author of *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*
- **Guido Imbens** — 2021 Nobel Laureate in Economics; Professor at Stanford GSB
- **Andrew Gelman** — Professor of Statistics and Political Science, Columbia University

**How to present this on ravikiran.us:** This is a strong credibility signal — published academic research co-authored with a Nobel laureate and leading industry practitioners, using Ravikiran's real-world eCommerce platform as the empirical site. Present it as a publication in the resume's publications or recognition section. On the homepage it can support the positioning around experimentation rigor and data-driven product management. Do not oversell Ravikiran's role as lead author — he is the final author (contributor), and his role was providing the empirical platform (SeaWorld) and co-authorship on the findings.
